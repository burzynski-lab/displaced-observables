\title{Displacement-aware jet substructure\\
for anomaly detection at the lifetime frontier}
\author{Jackson Burzynski}
\emailAdd{jackson.carl.burzynski@cern.ch}

\abstract{Dark-sector models with confining hidden dynamics predict emerging
jets: QCD-like jets whose constituents originate from displaced
dark-pion decays. Model-agnostic anomaly-detection searches built on
jet substructure, such as autoencoders trained on calorimeter- or
momentum-based observables, are largely blind to this signature. We
show that the standard substructure basis of angularities, energy
correlation functions, and $N$-subjettiness carries essentially no
lifetime information, so an anomalous sector whose only distinguishing
feature is displacement would evade substructure-based anomaly
detection. We introduce an interpretable, analytic family of
displacement-weighted jet substructure observables, comprising
$d_0$-weighted angularities, displaced energy--energy correlators, and
lifetime moments, and characterize their discrimination power as a
function of the dark-pion proper lifetime,
$c\tau(\pi_{\mathrm{D}}) \in [1, 300]$~mm, against light-flavor and
heavy-flavor QCD jets separately. The displacement-weighted track
multiplicity and the pairwise correlators dominate the single-track
observables precisely in the short-lifetime regime where dark pions
must be distinguished from $B$ hadrons. Augmenting the input basis of
an otherwise unchanged variational autoencoder with these observables
raises the signal efficiency at a fixed $1\%$ QCD anomaly rate from
below the percent level to $44\%$ at $c\tau = 1$~mm and above $88\%$
for $c\tau \geq 10$~mm, leaving the QCD background model unaffected,
and realizes about half to all of the fully supervised ceiling defined
by a boosted-decision-tree classifier on the complete observable
basis. The observables are robust against impact-parameter resolution
and pileup, and are inexpensive to add to existing anomaly-detection
architectures.}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Confining dark sectors coupled to the Standard Model through a heavy
mediator~\cite{Strassler:2006im} generically produce collider signatures in which a parton-level
dark quark showers and hadronizes into a multiplicity of dark hadrons,
a fraction of which decay back to SM particles with macroscopic proper
lifetimes~\cite{Schwaller:2015gea,Albouy:2022cin}. The resulting
``emerging jet'' contains an anomalously large fraction of tracks with
sizable impact parameter, distributed among several displaced vertices
along the jet axis.

Existing search strategies occupy two extremes. Cut-based analyses use
simple per-jet counting variables, such as the prompt-track energy
fraction or the median unsigned impact parameter, which are robust
and interpretable but discard the rich correlation structure of the
displaced final state~\cite{Schwaller:2015gea,ATLAS:EJ,ATLAS:EJRun2,CMS:EJ}.
Related dark-shower signatures in which part of the shower remains
invisible give rise to semi-visible jets~\cite{Cohen:2015toa}.
Machine-learned constituent-level taggers, at the other extreme,
approach the information-theoretic ceiling but resist physical
interpretation, complicate calibration, and obscure which features of
the signal carry the discrimination power.

A third, increasingly prominent strategy is \emph{model-agnostic
anomaly detection}, in which unsupervised or weakly supervised
architectures such as autoencoders (AEs), variational autoencoders
(VAEs), and density-based methods are trained on background-dominated
data to flag any jet or
event that populates the tails of the learned background
model~\cite{Farina:2018fyg,Heimel:2018mkt,Kasieczka:2021xcg,ATLAS:dijetAD}.
The generality of such searches is bounded by their input
representation: an anomaly can only be flagged if it is anomalous
\emph{in the variables the network sees}. Substructure-based anomaly
detection typically uses calorimeter- or momentum-based inputs, such
as constituent four-vectors, angularities, energy correlators, and
$N$-subjettiness, none of which carry lifetime information. This
paper demonstrates that blind spot quantitatively: across every
standard substructure observable we study, emerging jets are separated
from QCD by rejection factors of only $2$--$5$, independent of the
dark-pion lifetime (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}). A dark shower whose
principal anomaly is \emph{displacement} looks ordinary to a
substructure autoencoder. The throughline of this work is that
augmenting anomaly-detection input bases with a small set of
interpretable, displacement-aware observables closes this blind spot
at negligible architectural cost (Sec.~\ref{sec:anomaly}).

This paper develops the middle ground: a systematic, analytic family of
jet substructure observables in which each track is weighted by its
displacement significance. The family is organized in three tiers of
increasing structure, namely weighted angularities (single-track
moments), displaced energy--energy correlators (pairwise moments), and
lifetime moments (direct $\ctau$ estimators), and reduces smoothly to standard
track-based substructure in the prompt limit. Because track-level
observables are not infrared-and-collinear safe, a first-principles
description would proceed through track
functions~\cite{Chang:2013rca,Li:2021zcf}. We define our observables
with this correspondence in mind but defer calculability questions.
\todo{tighten IRC/track-function framing with theorist input}

Our physics goals are as follows.
(i) Define the observable family and identify which moments carry
lifetime information.
(ii) Map discrimination power as a function of $\ctau(\pid)$ against
light-flavor and heavy-flavor QCD jets separately. The irreducible
background at short lifetime is $b$ jets, since
$\ctau(B) \approx 0.5$~mm.
(iii) Demonstrate that a (V)AE anomaly detector trained on a standard
substructure basis gains sensitivity to displaced signatures across the
full $\ctau$ range when the input basis is augmented with the
displacement-weighted observables, without degrading its background
model.
(iv) Quantify how much of the fully supervised discrimination ceiling
on the complete observable basis individual observables retain.
(v) Establish robustness under a realistic parametrized tracking
response.

\section{Simulation and event selection}
\label{sec:simulation}

\subsection{Signal and background samples}

Signal events are generated with \textsc{Pythia}~8.312
\cite{Bierlich:2022pfr} using the Hidden Valley
module~\cite{Carloni:2010tw}: $s$-channel production
$pp \to Z' \to \qD\bar{\qD}$ at $\sqrt{s} = 13.6$~TeV with
$m_{Z'} = 1.5$~TeV, followed by a dark shower and hadronization with
$N_{\mathrm{gauge}} = 3$, running dark coupling with
$\Lambda_{\mathrm{D}} = 10$~GeV, $m_{\qD} = 10$~GeV,
$m_{\pid} = 5$~GeV, $m_{\rho_{\mathrm{D}}} = 20$~GeV, and vector
fraction $0.75$, following the unflavored benchmark of the Dark Showers
Task Force~\cite{Albouy:2022cin} and the original emerging-jets
proposal~\cite{Schwaller:2015gea}. Dark rhos decay promptly to dark
pion pairs, and dark pions decay to $d\bar d$ with proper lifetime scanned
over $\ctau(\pid) \in \{0, 1, 3, 10, 30, 100, 300\}$~mm, where $0$
denotes the prompt limit.

The background is QCD dijet production in the same $\hat p_T$ regime,
split by jet flavor at analysis level: a jet is labeled $b$ ($c$) if a
weakly-decaying bottom (charm) hadron with $\pT > 5$~GeV lies within
$\dR < 1.0$ of the jet axis. A dedicated hard-$b\bar b$ sample
augments the $b$-jet statistics. Per-flavor results are shape-based, so
the samples are combined freely.

\subsection{Jet and track selection}

Jets: anti-$k_t$, $R=1.0$, built from all visible final-state
particles. The two leading jets with
$500 < \pT < 1000$~GeV and $|\eta| < 2.5$ are analyzed.
Tracks: charged constituents with $\pT > 1$~GeV, $|\eta| < 2.5$, and
truth $|\dnought| < 300$~mm. The truth impact parameter is computed
from the production vertex in the straight-line approximation,
$\dnought = (v_x p_y - v_y p_x)/\pT$. In all discrimination results the
background jets are reweighted, per flavor, so that their $\pT$
spectrum matches the (lifetime-independent) signal jet spectrum.
Rejection factors therefore reflect shape differences in the
observables, not residual kinematic differences between the $Z'$ decay
and the QCD $\hat p_T$ slice.

\subsection{Parametrized tracking response}
\label{sec:tracking}

Detector effects are applied as a parametrized transform of the truth
track collection, so identical observable code runs on truth-level and
smeared inputs. The impact-parameter resolution is
\begin{equation}
  \sigma(\dnought) = a \oplus b/\pT, \qquad
  a = 10~\mu\mathrm{m}, \quad b = 70~\mu\mathrm{m}\,\mathrm{GeV},
\end{equation}
and $\sigma_i$ in Eq.~(\ref{eq:weight}) always refers to this
parametrization, including at truth level. The longitudinal impact
parameter $z_0$, computed at the transverse point of closest approach,
is smeared analogously with
$\sigma(z_0) = 20~\mu\mathrm{m} \oplus 100~\mu\mathrm{m}\,\mathrm{GeV}/\pT$.
Reconstruction-efficiency effects, in particular the loss of efficiency
with increasing production radius and the role of dedicated
large-radius tracking, depend strongly on detector and reconstruction
details. A careful treatment of these efficiency considerations is
left to future work, and the results presented here include the
resolution smearing and the track selection below but assume fully
efficient tracking within the $|\dnought| < 300$~mm acceptance.

Pileup is modeled by overlaying Poisson($\mu$) minimum-bias events
(\textsc{Pythia} \texttt{SoftQCD:inelastic}) per jet before the tracking
transform, with each overlaid vertex displaced longitudinally by the
beam-spot spread ($\sigma_z = 45$~mm). Results are presented for
$\mu = 60$, representative of LHC Run~3 conditions, and compared to
the $\mu = 0$ baseline. At $\mu = 60$ the overlaid component amounts
to $11\%$ of the selected signal-jet tracks after the selection
below. Overlaid tracks receive the
identical smearing, efficiency, and selection as hard-scatter tracks.
A track is kept if it is
primary-vertex associated ($|z_0 \sin\theta| < 1.5$~mm) or is a
displaced candidate ($|\dnought|/\sigma > 3$). Prompt pileup from other
vertices fails both conditions. The surviving contamination is the
genuinely displaced pileup component from strange-hadron decays.

\section{Observable definitions}
\label{sec:observables}

Jets are clustered with the anti-$k_t$ algorithm~\cite{Cacciari:2008gp}
as implemented in \textsc{FastJet}~\cite{Cacciari:2011ma}
with $R = 1.0$ from all visible final-state particles. Observables are
computed from the jet's charged constituents (``tracks'') $i$ with
transverse-momentum fraction $z_i = p_{\mathrm{T},i} / \pT^{\mathrm{jet}}$, angle
$\theta_i = \dR(i, \mathrm{jet\ axis})/R$, transverse impact parameter
$d_{0,i}$, and per-track resolution $\sigma_i$.

\obshead{Displacement weight}

Each track carries a displacement weight based on its impact-parameter
\emph{significance},
\begin{equation}
  w_i \;=\; \ln\!\left(1 + \frac{|d_{0,i}|}{\sigma_i}\right).
  \label{eq:weight}
\end{equation}
The logarithm compresses the long lifetime tail, and the significance
ratio makes the weight self-regulating against resolution: a prompt
track smeared to $|\dnought| \sim \sigma$ receives $w \simeq \ln 2$,
while genuinely displaced tracks receive $w \sim \ln(|\dnought|/\sigma)
\gg 1$. In the ideal-detector limit $\sigma \to 0$ the weight diverges
only logarithmically, so all observables below remain finite and
smearing-stable.

Each family below is presented as a \emph{nominal} (lifetime-blind)
observable next to its \emph{displacement-weighted} counterpart,
obtained by a single insertion of the weight of Eq.~(\ref{eq:weight})
per track. This pairing is used throughout the paper: every
distribution, ROC, and anomaly-detection input basis is shown for both
members, so the lifetime information contributed by the weighting is
read off directly. Table~\ref{tab:observables} summarizes the family.

\obshead{Angularities}

The nominal track angularities~\cite{Larkoski:2014pca}, part of the
standard jet substructure toolkit~\cite{Larkoski:2017jix}, and their
displacement-weighted generalization are
\begin{equation}
  \lambda^\kappa_\beta \;=\; \sum_{i \in \mathrm{jet}}
      z_i^\kappa \, \theta_i^\beta ,
  \qquad\qquad
  \lambda^\kappa_\beta(w) \;=\; \sum_{i \in \mathrm{jet}}
      w_i \, z_i^\kappa \, \theta_i^\beta .
  \label{eq:angularity}
\end{equation}
The nominal family contains widely used substructure observables:
girth $(\kappa,\beta) = (1,1)$, the mass-like angularity $(1,2)$,
track multiplicity $n_{\mathrm{trk}} = \lambda^0_0$, and
$(\pT^D)^2 = \lambda^2_0$. The corresponding weighted anchors are the
displaced-$\pT$ fraction $(1,0)$, displaced girth $(1,1)$, and a
displaced-mass proxy $(1,2)$. In the fully prompt limit every track
sits at the resolution pedestal $w_i \simeq \ln 2$, so
$\lambda^\kappa_\beta(w) \to \ln 2 \cdot \lambda^\kappa_\beta$: the
weighted observable degenerates to a rescaled copy of its nominal
partner, and any \emph{shape} difference between the pair is a pure
lifetime effect. A scan in $(\kappa, \beta)$ identifies which moments
carry the lifetime information.
Figure~\ref{fig:kbscan} shows the $(\kappa,\beta)$ scan of the weighted
angularities: $b$-jet rejection at 50\% signal efficiency over the
grid $\kappa, \beta \in [0, 2]$. The result is a monotone gradient
toward the origin: the best moment is
$\lambda^0_0(w) = \sum_i w_i$, the displacement-weighted track
multiplicity, and every power of $z_i$ or $\theta_i$ \emph{dilutes} the
discrimination. Physically, the lifetime information resides in the
\emph{number} of significantly displaced tracks. Dark-pion decay
products are numerous but soft and broadly distributed within the jet,
so energy weighting suppresses exactly the tracks that carry the
signal. The single-observable hierarchy is therefore: weighted multiplicity
$\sum_i w_i$ $\gtrsim$ pairwise correlation (dEEC) $\gg$
energy-weighted angularities $\gg$ single-track means, with the dEEC
ahead against light jets at the shortest lifetimes. This motivates the benchmark set carried into
Secs.~\ref{sec:anomaly}--\ref{sec:supervised}: $\{\mathrm{dEEC}(\min),\;
\sum_i w_i,\; \lambda^1_0(w)\}$.

\obshead{Energy--energy correlators}

The nominal two-point energy correlation
function~\cite{Larkoski:2013eya} and its displaced counterpart are
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{ECF}_2(\beta) \;=\; \sum_{i<j} z_i z_j \, (\dR_{ij})^\beta,
  \qquad\qquad
  \mathrm{dEEC}(\beta) \;=\; \sum_{i<j} z_i z_j \,
      (\dR_{ij})^\beta \; g(w_i, w_j),
  \label{eq:deec}
\end{equation}
with pair weighting $g = w_i w_j$ or $g = \min(w_i, w_j)$.
The physics carried by the weighting is qualitatively new at this
order: dark-pion decays produce \emph{pairs} of displaced tracks from
common vertices, so displacement in signal is angularly correlated at
small pair separation $\dR_{ij}$, while in QCD (including $B$ decays,
with their single low-multiplicity vertex chain) the correlation is
weaker. The product weighting rewards pairs in which both tracks are
displaced. The $\min$ weighting is stricter, vanishing unless
\emph{both} tracks are significantly displaced, and is correspondingly
more robust against single-track resolution tails. The differential
profile $\langle w_i w_j \rangle (\dR_{ij})$, shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:wij}, exhibits the vertex pair correlation directly and
connects to the energy-correlator-on-tracks literature
\cite{Chang:2013rca,Jaarsma:2023ell}.

The construction extends to higher point count. Writing
$\mathrm{dECF}_1 = \sum_i z_i w_i$ and
$\mathrm{dECF}_2 = \mathrm{dEEC}$, the displaced three-point correlator
is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{dECF}_3(\beta) \;=\; \sum_{i<j<k} z_i z_j z_k \,
      (\dR_{ij}\dR_{ik}\dR_{jk})^\beta \; g(w_i, w_j, w_k),
\end{equation}
alongside the nominal $\mathrm{ECF}_3$ and ratios
$C_2 = \mathrm{ECF}_3 \mathrm{ECF}_1 / \mathrm{ECF}_2^2$,
$D_2 = \mathrm{ECF}_3 \mathrm{ECF}_1^3 / \mathrm{ECF}_2^3$. With the
product pairing $g = w_i w_j w_k$, each $\mathrm{dECF}_n$ carries $n$
powers of the weight, so in the displaced ratios
\begin{equation}
  C_2(w) = \frac{\mathrm{dECF}_3\,\mathrm{dECF}_1}{\mathrm{dECF}_2^2},
  \qquad
  D_2(w) = \frac{\mathrm{dECF}_3\,\mathrm{dECF}_1^3}{\mathrm{dECF}_2^3},
\end{equation}
the powers of $w$ cancel exactly as the powers of $z$ do: for a jet in
which all tracks share a common weight, $C_2(w)$ and $D_2(w)$ reduce
identically to their nominal partners. They are therefore
\emph{displacement-normalized} shape variables, insensitive to the
overall amount of displacement, and sensitive instead to its
angular and vertex \emph{structure}: a dark shower distributes
displacement over many vertices across the jet, while a $B$-decay
chain concentrates it in one.

\obshead{Lifetime moments}

The lifetime moments have no nontrivial nominal partner, since the
$\dnought$-independent limit of the family is
$L_0 = \sum_i z_i$, the charged energy fraction. They are the most
direct $\ctau$ estimators:
\begin{equation}
  L_n \;=\; \sum_i z_i \, |d_{0,i}|^n ,
  \qquad n = 0, 1, 2, \ldots
  \label{eq:moments}
\end{equation}
together with dimensionless ratios such as $L_2 L_0 / L_1^2$, which
measures the width of the per-jet displacement distribution
independently of its scale. These support a ``measure, don't just
tag'' program: given a tagged emerging-jet sample, the moments estimate

\obshead{Prong-structure observables and search-style baselines}

Two further sets complete the basis. First, the nominal
prong-structure observables, for which we do not construct weighted
counterparts here: $N$-subjettiness~\cite{Thaler:2010tr} with
exclusive-$k_t$ axes,
\begin{equation}
  \tau_N = \frac{\sum_i z_i \, \min_a (\dR_{ia})^\beta}
                {\sum_i z_i \, R^\beta},
\end{equation}
its ratios $\tau_{21}$, $\tau_{32}$, and the jet mass. Second, the
search-style single-track baselines against which any new displaced
observable must be compared: the mean impact-parameter significance
$\langle |\dnought|/\sigma \rangle$ and the prompt $\pT$ fraction
(fraction of the \emph{summed track} $\pT$ carried by tracks with
$|\dnought|/\sigma < 3$, normalized track-to-track rather than to
the jet $\pT$ so that jet-by-jet fluctuations of the neutral fraction
cancel), the latter analogous to the $\alpha$-type variables of
Refs.~\cite{Schwaller:2015gea,CMS:EJ}.

Every feature is z-score normalized, $\hat x = (x - \mu)/\sigma$, with
$\mu$ and $\sigma$ computed \emph{on the inclusive QCD sample only}.
Signal jets are normalized with the same constants, so displaced jets
appear many standard deviations from zero in the displaced features.
No other preprocessing is applied. In particular the heavy-tailed
features enter the autoencoder untransformed, since compressing their
tails was found to reduce the anomaly contrast.

Figure~\ref{fig:corr} shows the Pearson correlations of the displaced
features for QCD and signal. Three aspects are relevant for the
anomaly detection. First, the correlation \emph{structure} is itself
lifetime-sensitive: the angular-correlator block ($\lambda^1_1(w)$,
dEEC, $\mathrm{dECF}_3$) tightens markedly from QCD to signal
(e.g.\ $\rho[\lambda^1_1(w), \mathrm{dEEC}]$: $0.73 \to 0.90$),
because in signal these observables are fed by the same displaced
vertices. Since an autoencoder models the joint density, signal
jets that violate the learned QCD correlation pattern accrue
reconstruction error beyond their marginal outliers. Second, the
ratio observables ($C_2(w)$, $D_2(w)$, $L_2L_0/L_1^2$) are largely
decorrelated ($|\rho| \lesssim 0.4$) from the absolute-displacement
block in both samples, confirming that the vertex-structure
information enters the basis orthogonally. Third, two near-duplicate
pairs, $\mathrm{dEEC}(\mathrm{prod})/\mathrm{dEEC}(\min)$
($\rho \simeq 0.96$--$0.99$) and
$L_1/\langle|\dnought|/\sigma\rangle$ ($\rho \simeq 0.75$),
justify carrying only one of each into the benchmark observable set,
while the redundancy is harmless for the autoencoder inputs. (The
near-zero QCD correlations of $D_2(w)$ reflect its extreme tail
dominating the Pearson estimator rather than genuine independence.)

\subsection{Discrimination power versus lifetime}

Figure~\ref{fig:rejection} shows the background rejection at 50\%
signal efficiency as a function of $\ctau(\pid)$, per background
flavor. Three qualitative results emerge:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Multiplicity and pair correlation dominate the
  single-track baselines.} With $R = 1.0$ jets capturing most of the
  dark shower, the weighted multiplicity $\Sigma_i w_i$ is the
  strongest single observable against $b$ jets at every lifetime,
  reaching $385\times$ at $\ctau = 3$~mm and $54\times$ even at 1~mm,
  versus $\mathcal{O}(5)$ for the single-track means
  $\langle|\dnought|/\sigma\rangle$ and $L_1$, while the pairwise
  dEEC leads against light jets at the shortest lifetimes
  ($619\times$ at 1~mm). The displacement-normalized ratio $C_2(w)$
  shows a distinct and complementary behavior: its rejection is nearly
  \emph{independent of lifetime} ($\approx 10\times$ against $b$ jets
  for $1 \le \ctau \le 300$~mm), as expected for a variable sensitive
  to the vertex structure of the displacement rather than its amount.
  This provides a uniform working point across the entire lifetime
  grid.
  \item \textbf{Standard substructure carries no lifetime information.}
  All lifetime-blind observables sit at rejections of $2$--$5$,
  independent of $\ctau$, confirming that the discrimination above is
  attributable entirely to the displacement weighting. This is the
  quantitative statement of the anomaly-detection blind spot motivated
  in Sec.~\ref{sec:intro}: in any input basis built from these
  observables, emerging jets are at most mildly atypical, and no
  unsupervised method, however expressive, can recover
  information absent from its inputs.
  \item \textbf{The long-lifetime regime approaches saturation even at
  $10^5$-jet background samples.} With $1.2\times 10^5$ $b$ jets and
  $10^5$ light jets, rejections up to a few $\times 10^4$ are measured
  rather than bounded (e.g.\ $\Sigma_i w_i$ reaches a measured
  $b$-jet rejection of $3.2\times 10^4$ at $\ctau = 30$~mm), with
  the strongest entries at $\ctau \geq 100$~mm remaining statistics

\section{Anomaly detection with displacement-aware inputs}
\label{sec:anomaly}

The central application of the observable family is to extend the
generality of substructure-based anomaly detection to the lifetime
frontier. This section specifies the study completely: the datasets,
the input processing, the architectures and training procedure, and
the evaluation protocol. The comparison is deliberately constructed so
that the \emph{only} difference between the blind and the
displacement-aware anomaly detectors is the input basis.

\subsection{Datasets, input bases, and normalization}
\label{sec:addata}

The background training sample is the inclusive QCD dijet sample of
Sec.~\ref{sec:simulation}: $122\,258$ selected jets, split
$60/20/20$ into training ($73\,354$), validation ($24\,452$), and test
($24\,452$) subsets by a seeded random permutation. The autoencoders
never see signal: all $9\,126$ signal jets per $\ctau$ point are used
for evaluation only. The b-enriched sample ($108\,453$ $b$-labeled
jets) is likewise held out and used only to measure the heavy-flavor
composition of the anomaly tail.

Two per-jet input vectors are compared. Basis $S$ (12 features) is the
nominal column of Table~\ref{tab:observables}: girth, mass angularity,
$\mathrm{ECF}_2$, $\mathrm{ECF}_3$, $C_2$, $D_2$, $\tau_1$,
$\tau_{21}$, $\tau_{32}$, $\pT^D$, $n_{\mathrm{trk}}$, and jet mass.
Basis $S{+}D$ (24 features) appends the displacement-weighted column:
$\Sigma_i w_i$, $\lambda^1_0(w)$, $\lambda^1_1(w)$,
$\mathrm{dEEC}(\mathrm{prod})$, $\mathrm{dEEC}(\min)$,
$\mathrm{dECF}_3$, $C_2(w)$, $D_2(w)$, $L_1$, $L_2L_0/L_1^2$,
$\langle|\dnought|/\sigma\rangle$, and the prompt-$\pT$ fraction.

The distributions of every input feature, and the correlations of the
displaced features, are shown in Figs.~\ref{fig:appS}, \ref{fig:appD},
and~\ref{fig:corr} of Sec.~\ref{sec:results}.

\subsection{Architecture, loss, and training}
\label{sec:adtrain}

Both models are small fully connected autoencoders acting on the
normalized feature vector $\hat x \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ ($d = 12$ or
$24$):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Encoder}: $d \to 64 \to 32$ with ReLU activations,
  followed by two linear heads producing $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^{8}$ and
  $\log\sigma^2 \in \mathbb{R}^{8}$ (latent dimension 8).
  \item \textbf{Decoder}: $8 \to 32 \to 64 \to d$, ReLU between hidden
  layers, linear output. About $7\times 10^3$ parameters in total.
  \item \textbf{VAE}~\cite{Kingma:2013hel}: the latent code is sampled
  via the reparameterization trick during training, $z = \mu + \epsilon \odot
  \sigma$ with $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, and set to
  $z = \mu$ at evaluation. The per-jet loss is the ELBO with
  feature-averaged reconstruction error,
  \begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}(x) \;=\; \frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}
        (\hat x_j - \tilde x_j)^2
    \;-\; \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{8}\sum_{k=1}^{8}
        \left(1 + \log\sigma_k^2 - \mu_k^2 - \sigma_k^2\right),
    \label{eq:elbo}
  \end{equation}
  where $\tilde x$ is the reconstruction.
  \item \textbf{AE}: identical architecture with a deterministic
  latent ($z = \mu$ throughout) and the MSE term of
  Eq.~(\ref{eq:elbo}) alone.
\end{itemize}
Training uses Adam~\cite{Kingma:2014vow} with learning rate $10^{-3}$ and batch size 256 on
the QCD training subset, for at most 200 epochs with early stopping
(patience 15) on the validation loss, and the best-validation weights are
retained. With fixed seeds the four trainings converge at 19 (VAE,
$S$), 48 (VAE, $S{+}D$), 195 (AE, $S$), and 126 (AE, $S{+}D$) epochs.
The \textbf{anomaly score} of a jet is its per-sample loss
$\mathcal{L}(x)$ (for the AE, the MSE alone).

\subsection{Working points and results}
\label{sec:adresults}

Anomaly-score thresholds are defined as quantiles of the QCD
\emph{test} subset at fixed anomaly rates of $10^{-2}$ and $10^{-3}$.
The figure of merit is the signal efficiency at these fixed rates,
per $\ctau$ point.

Figure~\ref{fig:vaescore} shows the score distributions. In basis $S$
the signal is indistinguishable from QCD at every lifetime. The
efficiency at the $1\%$ working point is $0.2$--$0.5\%$,
consistent with the background rate, and zero to sample precision at
$10^{-3}$.
In basis $S{+}D$, with architecture, training, and working points
unchanged, the signal population migrates orders of magnitude into the
loss tail, and the efficiency at the $1\%$ working point becomes
$44\%$ at $\ctau = 1$~mm, $69\%$ at 3~mm, $88\%$ at 10~mm, and
$\geq 97\%$ for $\ctau \geq 30$~mm (VAE, Fig.~\ref{fig:vae}).

Figure~\ref{fig:vaerecon} shows \emph{why} through the per-feature
reconstructions: trained only on QCD, the decoder reconstructs the
QCD test sample faithfully in every feature, while signal jets,
whose displaced features lie far outside the learned manifold, are
reconstructed back onto it, producing the large per-jet loss that the
anomaly score keys on.

Three further observations:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The background model is stable under augmentation.}
  The $b$-jet anomaly rate at the $1\%$ inclusive working point rises
  only from $\sim 0.5\%$ (basis $S$) to $\sim 1.8\%$ (basis $S{+}D$).
  This is the anticipated mild heavy-flavor tail, usable as an in-situ
  validation region.
  \item \textbf{The variational architecture outperforms the
  deterministic AE at short lifetime} ($44\%$ versus $15\%$ at 1~mm at
  the $1\%$ working point): the KL term regularizes the background
  model against the broad displaced features that otherwise dominate
  the plain MSE objective.
  \item \textbf{The cost of model-independence is confined to short
  lifetimes.} The black curve in Fig.~\ref{fig:vae} is a
  fully supervised XGBoost classifier (Sec.~\ref{sec:supervised})
  trained per $\ctau$ point on the same $S{+}D$ basis and evaluated at
  the identical fixed-anomaly-rate working points, so the gap to the
  red curve isolates supervision alone. The unsupervised VAE realizes
  $45\%$ of the supervised efficiency at $\ctau = 1$~mm, $70\%$ at
  3~mm, $89\%$ at 10~mm, and $\geq 98\%$ for $\ctau \geq 30$~mm: at
  long lifetime the signal sits so deep in the tails of the learned
  QCD density that supervision buys nothing, while near the $B$-hadron
  lifetime a targeted search retains a genuine advantage.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Comparison with fully supervised classifiers}
\label{sec:supervised}

To quantify how much of the family's combined discrimination a single
interpretable observable retains, we compare against fully supervised
classifiers trained on the same features and jet splits. The ceiling
is an XGBoost~\cite{Chen:2016btl} classifier on the complete
24-feature $S{+}D$ basis,
trained separately per $\ctau$ point: signal versus inclusive QCD
using the training subsets of Sec.~\ref{sec:addata} ($73\,354$ QCD
and $5\,476$ signal jets), up to 400 trees with the library defaults
(maximum depth 6, learning rate 0.3), class-weighted by the training
imbalance (\texttt{scale\_pos\_weight} $\simeq 13$), with early
stopping after 20 rounds without validation-logloss improvement.
Scores are class probabilities, and working points use the same
definitions as elsewhere (50\% signal efficiency for rejections, or
fixed QCD anomaly rates in Sec.~\ref{sec:adresults}). An identically
configured classifier restricted to the 12-feature nominal basis $S$,
and the two strongest single observables ($\mathrm{dEEC}(\min)$ and
$\Sigma_i w_i$), are evaluated on the same test jets.

Figure~\ref{fig:gap} shows the outcome. The single observables capture
$40$--$100\%$ of the ceiling's log-rejection across the lifetime grid:
the weighted multiplicity leads against $b$ jets at every lifetime and
itself saturates the test-sample ceiling for $\ctau \geq 30$~mm, while
the dEEC leads against light jets at the shortest lifetimes. The
nominal-basis classifier is the supervised counterpart of the
anomaly-detection blind spot: supervised training does extract a
lifetime-\emph{independent} $\sim 40\times$ $b$-jet rejection from
shape information alone, but the curve never rises with $\ctau$.
Displacement is where all the lifetime discrimination lives, and two
interpretable observables transmit most of it.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We have introduced an interpretable, analytic family of
displacement-weighted jet substructure observables for dark-shower
signatures, obtained from standard track-based substructure by a single
insertion of the impact-parameter significance weight
$w_i = \ln(1 + |d_{0,i}|/\sigma_i)$ per track. The family comprises
weighted angularities, displaced energy--energy correlators, and
lifetime moments, together with displacement-normalized ratios that
probe the vertex structure of a jet independently of its overall
displacement. Characterized across dark-pion lifetimes from the prompt
limit to $\ctau = 300$~mm and against light-flavor and heavy-flavor
QCD jets separately, the weighted track multiplicity $\Sigma_i w_i$
and the pairwise correlators provide background rejections that reach
several hundred against $b$ jets already at $\ctau = 3$~mm and exceed
$10^4$ at long lifetime, while every nominal substructure observable
remains at rejection factors of a few, independent of lifetime. A
fully supervised classifier on the complete observable basis confirms
that two interpretable observables transmit most of the available
discrimination.

The lifetime-independence of the nominal substructure basis is the
central cautionary result. A dark shower whose distinguishing feature
is displacement is invisible to anomaly-detection architectures built
on calorimeter- or momentum-based substructure alone, and no increase
in model capacity can recover information that is absent from the
inputs. Augmenting the input vector of an otherwise unchanged
variational autoencoder with twelve displacement-weighted observables
raises the signal efficiency at a fixed $1\%$ QCD anomaly rate from
the background-consistent level to $44\%$ at $\ctau = 1$~mm and above
$88\%$ for $\ctau \geq 10$~mm, realizing about half to all of the
fully supervised ceiling depending on lifetime. The QCD background
model is essentially unchanged by the augmentation and the heavy-flavor
anomaly rate grows only mildly.

These results carry a simple practical message for the growing program
of substructure-based anomaly detection at the LHC. The
displacement-aware observables proposed here are inexpensive in every
relevant sense. They are twelve numbers per jet, computed from track
collections that already exist in standard event reconstruction, they
require no new detector information, no architectural changes, and no
retuning of the background model, and they add negligible
computational cost to either training or inference. Including them in
the input representations of autoencoder-based and related
model-agnostic searches would extend the generality of those searches
to the lifetime frontier, where some of the least constrained and most
theoretically motivated hidden sectors reside. We encourage future
anomaly-detection efforts to adopt displacement-aware inputs as a
default.

\section{Pileup robustness}
\label{app:robustness}

Figure~\ref{fig:pileup} shows the benchmark observables with and
without minimum-bias pileup overlay at $\mu = 60$, using the
methodology of Sec.~\ref{sec:tracking}. The rejection curves are
nearly unchanged across the lifetime grid. The mechanism is twofold.
The $z_0$ association removes the prompt pileup bulk, and the
significance weighting caps what leaks through at the resolution
pedestal $w \simeq \ln 2$. The surviving displaced pileup component
from strange-hadron decays is angularly uncorrelated and too rare to
populate displaced pairs, so the pairwise correlators are essentially
unaffected. Displacement-weighted substructure is therefore
pileup-robust by construction, in contrast to track-counting variables
whose denominators grow with every accepted soft track.

\section{Pythia Hidden Valley settings}
\label{app:settings}

The signal configuration used for all results in this paper is listed
below. The dark-pion lifetime (\texttt{4900111:tau0}, in mm) and the
random seed are set per grid point at run time. The background samples
use the same beam settings with \texttt{HardQCD:all} (inclusive
dijets) or \texttt{HardQCD:hardbbbar} ($b$-enriched) and
\texttt{PhaseSpace:pTHatMin = 450}, and the pileup library uses
\texttt{SoftQCD:inelastic}.

{\small
\begin{verbatim}
! Hidden Valley emerging-jet signal: s-channel Z' -> qv qvbar
! Benchmark following Schwaller-Stolarski-Weiler (1502.05409) / Dark Showers
! Task Force unflavored scenario, ATLAS emerging-jets-like mediator mass.
! ctau(pi_d) [4900111:tau0] and the random seed are set programmatically.

Beams:idA = 2212
Beams:idB = 2212
Beams:eCM = 13600.

! ---- hard process: s-channel Zv ----
HiddenValley:ffbar2Zv = on
4900023:m0 = 1500.
4900023:mWidth = 15.
4900023:onMode = off
4900023:onIfAny = 4900101

! ---- dark sector: SU(3), one light flavor, running coupling ----
HiddenValley:Ngauge = 3
HiddenValley:alphaOrder = 1
HiddenValley:Lambda = 10.
HiddenValley:FSR = on
HiddenValley:fragment = on
HiddenValley:pTminFSR = 11.
HiddenValley:probVector = 0.75

! ---- dark sector masses ----
4900101:m0 = 10.     ! dark quark qv
4900111:m0 = 5.      ! dark pion pi_d
4900113:m0 = 20.     ! dark rho rho_d (rho -> pi pi open)

! ---- decays back to SM ----
! rho_d -> pi_d pi_d (prompt); pi_d -> d dbar with lifetime set at runtime
4900113:oneChannel = 1 1.0 0 4900111 4900111
4900111:oneChannel = 1 1.0 91 1 -1
4900111:mayDecay = on

! ---- generic settings ----
Random:setSeed = on
Next:numberShowEvent = 0
Next:numberShowProcess = 0
Next:numberShowInfo = 0
Print:quiet = on
\end{verbatim}
